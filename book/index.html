The puzzle that started this book

A skeptic, a tree at Guindy, and a car that drove itself.

The engineer

At the College of Engineering, Guindy, Sundar trusted only what a slide rule could measure. His friends called him Quantum. Meditation was something for other people.

The encounter

Then a young PhD candidate, a scholar who knew more quantum mechanics than the lecturers, quietly took his arguments apart. Behind the engineering block, under a tree, something was passed on that Sundar never earned and couldn't explain.

The decades

A career in technology followed. So did a calm that friends noticed before he did. Meanwhile, people around him with far more discipline struggled to sit still for ten minutes. Why?

The missing variable

The answer arrived on a Los Angeles freeway, the day he finally let his Tesla drive. It clicked at a Supercharger on Guru Pūrṇimā.

What if meditation isn't something you force, but something you allow, like a car that was always able to drive itself?

Two ways to move the mind

Method A · The shepherd

Like a shepherd driving sheep into a pen. You bark orders, use a stick, force the sheep where they don't want to go. The moment you look away, they scatter.

This is Dhāraṇā: effortful concentration, an adversarial relationship with your own mind.

Method B · The bird

Like a bird coming home to roost at sunset. Nobody has to force it. It wants to go there. The bird isn't pushed; it's pulled.

This is Dhyāna, Pratyak Pravāhatā: the mind's natural flow inward. You don't drive the meditation; the meditation drives you.

Is this book religious?

It's a memoir by a former skeptic, not a sermon, and it doesn't ask you to convert. It draws on the Vedanta tradition and the author's own teacher. It also listens to Christian voices: Jesus at the Pool of Bethesda, Mary and Martha, and the medieval Christian classic The Cloud of Unknowing. The author's point is that sincere seekers in different traditions describe the same thing: effort can take you only so far, and what comes next arrives as grace. Readers can bring the ideas back to their own faith, or to none.

Do I need to meditate already?

No. The book starts from the most common experience of all: sitting down to be still and finding the mind won't cooperate.

The Self-Driving Mind / Book overview

An unexpected beginning.

College of Engineering, Guindy. A skeptical student nicknamed “Quantum”, obsessed with Feynman and pure logic, meets a young PhD candidate beside a Royal Enfield motorcycle.

The student expects a debate about religion. Instead, he meets someone who answers every objection with logic, on the student's own terms. That meeting begins a lifelong inquiry into meditation and grace.

Two people. One has every advantage discipline can buy. The other has almost none.

Subject AMaximum discipline. Color-coded calendars, everything managed with precision.“Even when I sit down, the connection just isn't there.”
Subject BMinimum discipline. Disorganized, pretty random.“I never found it too hard to connect when I wanted to.”

If discipline equals results, this can't be true. There has to be a missing variable. The book is the search for it.
