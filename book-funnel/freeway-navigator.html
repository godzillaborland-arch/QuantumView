Subscription vs. Endowment

Three miles of coastal freeway, two ways to drive it. Grip the wheel and every correction costs a little willpower: that’s the subscription. Let go, and the route you already set carries you: that’s the endowment.

What the words mean
Manual · Subscription
You hold the wheel. Every correction is paid for in willpower, like a subscription that bills by the mile.
Autonomous · Endowment
The destination was set before you started. Let go and the guidance carries you, refilling energy as it goes. The book uses a self-driving car as a stand-in for dhyāna, meditation that runs without white knuckles. That’s an analogy, not a product review.
Ego energy
Your willpower battery. Steering, braking and tailgating spend it. Letting go refills it. At zero the car only crawls.
Friction
How tense the road feels: cars too close, jerky corrections, crowds. It makes energy drain faster. Zero when you let go.
Clarity
How clearly the guidance can see. Every so often grime builds up while it drives. Until you wipe, the car slows to 20 mph and stops refilling energy. I read this as yama and niyama, the ethical groundwork that keeps perception clear. My reading, not a ruling.
Roadside notes
Green signs with lines from the book. When the guidance is driving you have the attention to read the small print; when you’re steering, it goes by. All of them wait for you on the results screen.
Controls
