Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

Retail Economics & The B&N 300 Rule

Amazon takes roughly 65% discount, Barnes & Noble ~50%, and indie stores ~40%. Hitting 300 pre-orders on B&N is the key threshold that influences their decision to physically stock your book in stores.

Retail Phrasing

Always push brick-and-mortar publicly. Tell your audience the book is “available wherever books are sold” and say “international bestseller” rather than “Amazon bestseller.” Never direct bulk sales to Amazon—they may ship 200 books in 200 individual boxes.

Bookmarks & ARCs

Print bookmarks with a book thumbnail, short description, and QR code pointing to your pre-order site (limit ~250 for events). Distribute Advance Reader Copies with special stickers to make early readers feel special and build launch momentum.

The “Bloom Crew” Strategy

Build an early waitlist and convert them into a launch crew. Give them a shareable digital kit and ask them shamelessly to request your book at regional libraries.

AI Copyright Warning

You can use Grok or ChatGPT for brainstorming and sentence clarity, but AI-generated content cannot be legally copyrighted. Your book must remain distinctly written by you.

The “Five Things” Rule

Do not get overwhelmed trying every marketing tactic at once. Consistently execute just three to five marketing actions a week—that alone puts you ahead of the majority of authors.

Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

The “Two Bridges” of Marketing

Build the Authority Bridge by sharing your publishing journey the moment you decide to write. Once you’ve invested the audience early, crossing the Permissions Bridge (asking them to pre-order) happens automatically.

Retail Placements & Ebook Promos

Drop your ebook price to $0.99, $1.99, or $2.99 during publication week to drive high-volume sales and accidentally hit bestseller lists.

Hudson’s Airport Placement

You can pay ~$5,000 for Hudson’s Airport physical cover-out placement, or simply leverage their free website listing for brand credibility at no cost.

The Blank Book Photo Hack

If your physical book isn’t printed yet, hold a blank white book in your photos. Use AI or Photoshop to overlay your cover onto it afterward for polished marketing materials.

LinkedIn Algorithm Hack

Simply “liking” fellow authors’ posts does not boost the algorithm. You must write a comment on their LinkedIn posts to trigger the algorithmic push.

Avoid Download Codes

One-time-use digital download codes are clunky. It’s far more efficient to set up an autoresponder that sends the direct landing page link to your readers automatically.

Maximize Cover Reveals

A cover reveal with a countdown generates massive early traffic and can even attract potential sponsors for your launch.

Sell Without Selling

At workshops, weave your book into a story and attach a free coaching session for anyone who pre-orders on the spot.

Amazon Review Milestones

25 reviews unlocks better co-op advertising. 50 reviews unlocks A+ content. At 100 reviews, Amazon begins spending their own money on keyword advertising for your book.

The Endorsement Hack

Pre-write 6–12 sample endorsements and let the endorser simply pick a number or tweak it slightly. This dramatically increases your success rate by removing the effort barrier.

Hiring a Publicist

Hire a publicist only as a “designated gorilla” with strict boundaries. Without a backend monetization funnel (e.g., a $197 product), you will likely spend more than you earn in royalties.

PodMatch

Use mypodmatch.com to find and book targeted podcast shows directly—rather than paying a publicist to handle outreach.

Physical vs. Digital Perceived Value

Physical books carry significantly more perceived value and authority in funnels than ebooks. Weight matters.

Tax Compliance at Live Events

Check with your local CPA regarding your state’s specific laws for collecting sales tax when selling author copies at live events.

Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

The Book Signing “Crowd” Illusion

Never sit or stand behind your signing table—always stand out in front of it to engage people. Bring at least three people who love you to the event and keep two of them standing in line talking to you at all times to create an immediate illusion of high demand.

The “Wandering Shill” Bookstore Tactic

Task a third friend or family member to wander the store carrying an autographed copy of your book. Have them actively talk you up to other shoppers (“Oh my gosh, I can’t believe this author is here today—you have to go check them out!”) to physically herd traffic over to your line.

Inscription Efficiency Rule

Do not try to write custom, ad-libbed messages on the fly at a busy signing—you will inevitably start writing down the words you are speaking and ruin the book. Memorize a fixed, standard phrase in advance. Write the reader’s name, insert your standard phrase, sign it, and explicitly date it. Have readers write their names on a 3×5 notepad first to avoid spelling errors.

The Retail “Trunk Trick”

Bookstores under-order event stock constantly. Never go to a signing without a backup case of books hidden in your vehicle trunk. If the store sells out, tell the manager you can supply your own books—they will facilitate the sales on-site and reimburse you by ordering replacement stock from the publisher later.

The 3-Visit Retail Relationship Strategy

Do not walk into a bookstore cold and pitch a signing—you will be met with an automatic “no.” Instead, use a three-step pipeline:

Visit 1: Browse the store, buy something small, make friends with a staff member (ideally the Community Relations Manager), and leave.
Visit 2: Return a week or two later while the connection is warm, repeat the friendly interaction, buy something small, and leave.
Visit 3: Explain that you are a locally distributed author and pitch the signing as a collaborative event where you are bringing your entire network into their store to buy products.

Media-Driven Signing Bookings

Proactively pitch local print, radio, and TV stations 60 days before a planned event. Secure a short media interview snippet, then leverage that media exposure to prove to the bookstore manager you are bringing guaranteed traffic.

Leveraging Regional Store Managers

Once you execute a successful signing at one location, call the store manager in your next target market. Name-drop the manager you just worked with to establish instant internal retail credibility.

Post-Launch Textual Error Corrections

The publisher retains massive flexibility for interior text updates post-launch. Submit a support ticket and the publisher will update the digital master files so all future printings are completely corrected.

Custom Audiobook Strategy

Audiobooks do not have to be recorded word-for-word if standard prose reading doesn’t fit your format. You can record an abbreviated custom audio walkthrough guiding listeners through the concepts, paired with downloadable companion worksheets or PDFs.

Strategic Audio Delay

Release your audiobook 3 months after your print publication date. This keeps early audio sales from cannibalizing the initial print pre-orders needed for physical bookstore shelf placement, while giving you a powerful secondary “launch week” milestone to promote later.

Pre-Pub Bulk Sales & Bestseller Stacking

If an organization or business wants to buy multiple copies before your official publication date, coordinate the purchase through Beantown Books. They will hold the physical reporting data and stack it directly into your launch week numbers to maximize your shot at hitting major bestseller lists.

Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

Audiobook Production

Always record in your own voice to build listener connection. If impossible, use Futuri Books—a human-managed AI voice clone service for around $2,200.

PodMatch Proactivity

Do not wait for hosts to reach out. Proactively message them and tweak your profile at least once every three months. Repurpose podcast interviews into short video clips for Instagram to prove you are a good guest.

Chamber of Commerce Limo Tour

Pitch local Chamber of Commerce branches for speaking gigs, or rent a limo and do a tour across different cities for maximum exposure.

The Self-Shipping Hack

Buy a roll of 3M Scotch bubble wrap packaging instead of envelopes and tape. The material sticks to itself, so you wrap a book, trim, slap on a label, and ship instantly.

Bookstore Signings

Never show up without a backup case of your own books—stores frequently sell out or order late. Request a one-time consignment deal if necessary.

Amazon “Browsing” Algorithm

Amazon categorizes your book based on what the user was browsing before they clicked your link. Request a global metadata refresh if you end up falsely ranked in an unrelated category.

Instant Bestseller Verification

If you rank under 100 in any Amazon category, you are officially an Amazon Bestseller. Take a screenshot immediately—Amazon will not notify you.

Frankfurt Book Fair Warning

Skip offers (e.g., $2,000) to feature your book at Frankfurt. It is a rights event for publishers, not a consumer fair—expect zero consumer sales.

The “Free Content” Hack

Never offer a “Free Chapter”—readers assume you’ll pressure them to buy. Call it “Free Content” or a “Free Report” instead and capture their email with less resistance.

Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

Bestseller List Mechanics

USA Today requires sales across at least 3 different sources (Amazon, B&N, Indies), but weighs indie stores significantly heavier. Pre-pub week is the easiest time to hit it.

Barnes & Noble Bestseller

You must manually scroll the top 100 category lists to find your book and screenshot it—B&N does not display category ranks prominently or notify you.

The Blacklist Warning

Barnes & Noble actively blacklists authors (sometimes for up to 5 years) for promoting Amazon too heavily on public platforms. Never highlight Amazon over B&N in your marketing.

The “Memorized Tagline” Signing Hack

Come up with a short, personalized tagline to write in every book you sign. This lets you write smoothly while talking to readers, preventing you from accidentally inscribing their conversation.

Book Signing Mechanics

Use blue ink to make the signature pop. Keep backup pens in a baggie. Use a fishbowl or iPad form to capture emails. Have readers write their name on a notepad first to avoid misspelling unique names.

Interior Typo Fixes

Post-publication typos or textual errors can be fixed—submit a ticket and the publisher will update the file for future printings and append the copyright date to indicate the corrected version.

Royalty Delay

Royalty payments have a 9-month delay to account for bookstore returns, which average under 15%.

Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

The Bestseller Pre-Order Strategy

To convince national reps to stock your book on physical shelves, aim to drive 300 pre-orders directly to Barnes & Noble’s website to signal strong reader demand. However, if your goal is hitting the USA Today or New York Times bestseller lists, you must spread pre-orders across at least three different retailers (e.g., Barnes & Noble, Beantown Books, Books-A-Million).

Author Copies vs. Bulk Bestseller Orders

The 2,000 author copies you buy directly from Morgan James are provided at base print cost ($3–$5), but these do not count toward any bestseller lists. If you want a bulk-sale campaign that officially counts toward lists, you must route those orders through Beantown Books (at wholesale pricing), which does report the sales.

Strategic E-Book Pricing (99-Cent Promos)

Submit the price-drop request form 1–2 weeks in advance. Do not run a 99-cent e-book promo during the week of your physical book launch—it devalues the print edition. Instead, run the discount either just before launch to drive pre-orders, or a month after launch to spike your Amazon rankings.

Report Your Marketing Activity

Consistently submit the “marketing updates form” for all of your activities—TikToks, podcast episodes, speaking gigs, op-eds. The publisher forwards these updates to the sales and distribution team every week; they use this proof of momentum as leverage to convince bookstore buyers to stock your book.

Hudson’s Airport Placement Facts

If you pursue airport placement, opt for the Trade Paperback rather than the Hardcover—it is more cost-effective and gets your book into more locations (~110 airports vs. ~80). The large co-op fee (around $15,000) does include the printing cost of the books that will be stocked.

Book Interior Limitations: Images & Color

Keep interior diagrams and images to a strict maximum of 20–25. Avoid partial color printing entirely—presses cannot print just a few color pages; it is “all or nothing.” Adding color drastically increases print cost and forces your retail price up. Once your book enters the interior design phase, limit changes to minor aesthetic tweaks only.

Host Value-Driven Launch Events

Frame your launch event around the value the reader will receive rather than calling it a basic “book launch” (e.g., “A Morning of Hope, Healing, and Inspiration”). Readers who pre-ordered online will likely receive their copies right around the pub date—they can bring those to be signed and buy extra copies at the event to give as gifts.

Podcast “Evergreen” Rule

Because podcasts may be discovered by a listener years in the future, always speak on them as if your book is already published—even if you are currently in the pre-order phase.

Shipping Costs Will Eat Your Margins

Author copies are cheap to print but fast shipping is brutally expensive. One author noted overnight/2-day shipping for a carton of books cost $300, whereas standard 3–4 week shipping was only $20. Plan ahead to avoid paying for expedited freight.

Strict Production Timelines

To properly execute a launch with ARCs and marketing runway, finish production 6–9 months before your publication date. The absolute minimum is 3 months—anything under that threshold requires urgent intervention with the team.

Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

B&N Print Pre-order Rule

eBook pre-orders on Barnes & Noble do not count toward the crucial 300 pre-order benchmark required for physical bookstore shelf distribution. B&N prioritizes physical book pre-orders because stocking print inventory involves a very different financial investment than digital delivery.

Amazon Subcategory Rankings

Amazon uses a combination of categories and keywords to index books. Using narrower, niche subcategories makes it far easier to rise quickly in rankings—with a niche category (such as “southern psychological thrillers”), a book can hit a bestseller list with just a few sales in an hour or a day. Any author whose book ranks in the top 100 of any subcategory can claim the title of a “best-selling author.”

Campaign Category Customization

Metadata and categories are selected professionally by the MJP team. They aren’t always shared by default to avoid unnecessary revisions, but the team will share them on request—reach out directly if you plan a specific promotional campaign that needs niche categories adjusted at the right time.

Ad Budget Testing Strategy ($500–$1,000)

Never launch blind ads. Test your headlines first by spending $50 over three or four days. Once you have a proven headline, test the ad content and scale up incrementally—starting at $50 and not exceeding $250 until the ad is performing well.

Facebook Value-Add Funnel

Avoid running Facebook ads that only try to sell your book directly. Instead, run a targeted ad that gives away a free high-value resource taken directly from your book—this collects email addresses, pulls readers into your marketing ecosystem, and converts them later.

Review Threshold for Ads

Do not run paid Amazon ads until your book has gathered at least 50 to 100 reviews. Without reviews to prove social credibility, traffic driven to your page will not convert into sales.

BookBub & Discount Form Scheduling

If you secure a BookBub date, submit Morgan James’s eBook Promotion Request Form at least two weeks in advance so the team can schedule the ebook discount. MJP updates the pricing feed about three days before the campaign so the discounted rate propagates cleanly to Amazon, Barnes & Noble, and other major platforms.

Unsecured PDF Landing Page Warning

Free ebook landing pages typically deliver the file as an unsecured PDF or EPUB that anyone can download and share freely. If pitching organizations for bulk physical sales, sharing a full unsecured PDF can devalue your book, since organizations might simply forward the free PDF to all event attendees.

Landing Page Lead Magnet Alternative

If you have piracy concerns, don’t offer the full book. Instead, use a single sample chapter, free checklist, or specialized workbook kit as your landing page lead magnet to collect contact details.

August Copyright Strategy

Books released in late August can qualify for a 2027 copyright date rather than 2026, preserving the “freshness” of the title in the market for the coming year.

Endorsement Timelines

Potential endorsers are accustomed to reading raw Word documents or protected PDFs early in the publishing process. If needed, Morgan James can hold your book in Phase 2 for up to three weeks to integrate late-arriving high-level endorsements directly into the printed layout.

The 3-Month Out-of-Production Safety Buffer

To safely print, ship, and coordinate launch efforts, authors should be completely out of the production phase at least three to six months (ideally nine months) before their public publication date.

Local Author Bio Hook

MJP strategically includes your city of residence in your author bio because physical bookstores are significantly more likely to stock and promote books by local authors.

Marketing Tips & Tactical Hidden Gems

Media Outreach Lead Times

Begin pitching and scheduling media appearances four to five months before your publication month to account for the lead times required by podcasts, journals, and local TV programs.

Targeted Business Outlets

For business titles, focus on business and entrepreneurship podcasts, industry association podcasts/newsletters, trade publications, local business journals, local newspapers, and chamber of commerce networks. Favor high-quality, targeted fits over sheer quantity.

TV Theme Month Strategy

Television lifestyle and morning show producers schedule segments around seasonal themes and “national awareness months” (e.g., National Mental Health Awareness Month). Ethically map your book’s topic to fit these themes to help producers fill their recurring calendar segments.

Spoon-Feed Segment Pitches

TV producers ignore long pitches and standard press releases. Send a highly concise “sample segment” hook and provide at least three pre-written interview questions so the host can run the segment with zero effort.

The 3-Minute Airworthiness Rule

To book TV, provide a one-to-three-minute video clip proving you can hold air, smile, and speak clearly on screen. Skip fancy graphic intros or lower-thirds—producers just want to see an authentic human being.

“Fail Forward with Finesse” in Smaller Markets

Never pitch your home television market first. Pitch a smaller regional station 30 to 60 minutes away to practice, desensitize yourself, and build a video track record. This protects your relationship with local producers, who often stay at major home affiliate stations for many years.

Launch Day “Pull Out Your Phone” Bestseller Hack

At your physical launch or reading, display a prominent QR code. Ask the audience to physically pull out their phones and purchase the eBook for 99 cents right there to instantly spike your Amazon bestseller ranking.

International Currency Price Updates

Use the eBook Promotion Form to temporarily discount your eBook to 99 cents, applying the discount only to specific international currencies (such as India, the UK, and the Philippines) while keeping the full retail price active in the US. Updates take 1 to 2 days to propagate.

Physical Case Printing Lead Times

Order physical books at least 30 days (ideally 60 days) in advance of a live event. Expediting print runs is much cheaper than expediting shipping costs.

Forgoing the Print Proof

If facing a tight timeline, you can forgo the physical proof copy to speed up delivery by one to two weeks, though you accept the minor risk of formatting or printing shifts.

PR Pitch Personalization (Ethical Stalking)

When pitching media, research individual journalists or hosts on social media to identify their personal hobbies, interests, or family experiences. Use this to build a highly tailored, human-to-human connection.

The “Blank Book Wrap” Prop Hack

TV producers only care that you are the legitimate author and can speak clearly. If your book hasn’t finished printing, take your approved, high-resolution cover jacket, wrap it around a blank book of similar size, and bring it on set as a physical prop.

The National Media Catch-22

Major national networks (such as CNN or CBS) will almost never book an author who hasn’t already proven themselves on local TV first. Always establish a track record of local TV appearances and include those video links in your pitches to national news directors.

Finding Unlisted Producer Emails

If a lifestyle producer’s direct email is unlisted, call the television station’s main phone line, speak to the human operator, and ask for their direct email address or a general department email to direct the pitch.
